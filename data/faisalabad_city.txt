Faisalabad: City Profile

Faisalabad is a city in the Punjab province of Pakistan. It is the third
most populous city in Pakistan and a major industrial centre, especially
known for its textile industry. The city is sometimes called the
"Manchester of Pakistan" because of the scale of its textile production.

The city was originally named Lyallpur during the British colonial period.
It was renamed Faisalabad in 1977. One of its most famous landmarks is the
Ghanta Ghar, a clock tower built during the colonial era. Eight roads
radiate outwards from the clock tower, and the layout of the bazaars
around it is said to resemble the Union Jack flag when seen from above.

Faisalabad has a semi-arid climate. Summers are very hot, with
temperatures that can exceed 40 degrees Celsius in May and June. Winters
are mild and short, with January temperatures often between 5 and 20
degrees Celsius. Most of the city's rainfall arrives during the monsoon
season in July and August.

The University of Agriculture, Faisalabad, founded in 1906, is one of the
oldest and largest agricultural universities in Pakistan. The city's
economy also includes chemical plants, engineering workshops, and a large
dry port that handles imports and exports for the region.
